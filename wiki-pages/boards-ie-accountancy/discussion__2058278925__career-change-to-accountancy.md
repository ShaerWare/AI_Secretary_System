### Post by Jim2007 (2010-05-24T15:19:49+00:00)

The world of accounting is a very big field, a lot depends on what you want to achieve.  Do you want to work in practice or in industry.  In  practice would want to develop a career in audit, tax, advisory services, consulting etc and like wise in industry to what to work in the financial or fiduciary area or more in cost and management accounting.  On the industrial side the other question would be what sector would you like to work in - something in the services area or in the manufacturing/construction/fabrication area.And what is the long term objective - a partner in a large practice or running your own practice, the CFO of a company, another senior management position and so on.

I can't speak for others, but my opinion that college is a waste of time for mature accounting students. Better to look for accounting or accounting technician professional (not academic) studies. You can join one of the accounting (accounting technician) bodies AAT, CAT, CTA, ATI or jump straight into ACCA or CIMA and work with it. Currently Springboard has openings and a few days left to apply - check their website, type in the search 'ACCA'. Watch Youtube 'how to become an accountant'. Read Google AAT vs ACCA, CIMA vs ACCA etc. There will be less info about CTA or ATI as they are not very globally known because they are mainly operating basically just in Ireland.Some people do AAT (or ATI, or CAT), then get some exemptions and continue with ACCA, then take ATT for a deeper knowledge of taxation. Some stick with just one of them. Some go to academic studies (business/finance) and then get exemptions to some ACCA exams and then do the rest of them once they graduate from their college... Just make sure you ask the college about the ACCA exemptions, if you'd go this way. I personally find ACCA very exciting, however, challenging as my background is completely different. But if you have a good grasp and are motivated, then ACCA in the evenings/weekends is possible even working full time. However, I found doing one of the Springboard courses is best for me, as they give some sort of a structure, discipline and certainly professional guidance.If you don't feel confident, then do free courses firstly. They would show you if you like it or not. And if you do, then they would build a solid basic understanding which could help making more serious steps into college or one of the above accountancy bodies.Springboard.Application deadline 30/01/2023https://springboardcourses.ie/details/10854Application deadline 24/02/2023https://springboardcourses.ie/details/10865
